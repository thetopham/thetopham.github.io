Marinara · Animation in development

We already write scripts in our roleplays. Why not bring them to life?

I contribute to Marinara Engine and Marinara Agents, an AI roleplay and storytelling project. Right now I’m working on a MiniMax H3 workflow in ComfyUI, a visual tool for connecting AI image and video steps.

The dream is to watch our stories become animation and anime that grow with the roleplay, instead of leaving them as text in a chat.

From a roleplay to an animated scene

  1. Keep the characters recognizable. Each character has a reference image from their character sheet, plus their persona or written description.
  2. Keep the places familiar. World Maps supplies location reference images. The aim is consistent backgrounds, including places that look like the original fictional setting when we’re roleplaying in an existing fandom.
  3. Use the story we already wrote. The roleplay narration becomes the script. AI turns it into a storyboard—a scene-by-scene animation plan.
  4. Prepare the pictures and movement. Character and location references guide the keyframes, the still images that anchor each shot. AI also writes instructions for the action and camera movement.
  5. Make the animation. ComfyUI brings the reference material, keyframes, and motion instructions into the video workflow. MiniMax H3 is the local animation experiment I’m working on now.

Where it stands: The pieces are being connected and refined. Consistent characters, familiar locations, and good action are goals—not a promise of a finished, one-click anime generator. I’d love to get closer to Seedance-style action quality on my own hardware; I haven’t demonstrated that comparison.

Manfred · Wearable prototype

AI ears and eyes for everyday life.

Manfred is my wearable memory project. An Omi wearable provides daily audio for transcription and lifelogging: a searchable record of conversations and moments I want to remember.

I’m also connecting E09 smart glasses to ChatGPT Live for voice conversations and photo uploads. The idea is to talk to AI through the glasses and share what I’m looking at, without stopping to type everything into my phone.

Where it stands: The glasses work for voice, and photo sharing has been tested. I’m still making capture and delivery more reliable; this isn’t a finished always-on assistant.

Recording needs to respect the people around me. Personal recordings are not published here.

Code and technical details

Android/Flutter companion, Bluetooth audio, Python transcription and search, upload buffering, and Tasker phone automation.

Demerzel · My AI helper

The helper behind the scenes.

Demerzel brings together daily AI briefs, a personal wiki built from my daily conversations, and management of the computers that run my AI projects.

The wiki is a growing, organized notebook for ideas, project notes, and useful context. The briefs give me a daily overview. The fleet tools help manage which AI programs run on which machine.

The home AI fleet

“Fleet” just means the group of computers I use together:

AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700 system
Larger local AI models and MiniMax H3 animation experiments, switching between jobs that share the graphics card.
Dual NVIDIA RTX 3060 system
Local language models and a separate machine for coding and research experiments.
Single NVIDIA RTX 3060 system
ComfyUI image-generation workflows.

The management tools check what is running, start or stop supported programs, and keep jobs from fighting over the same graphics card.

The separate automatic coding/research workers are currently paused while I check recent changes. That is one part of Demerzel—not its whole purpose. The wiki, recordings, and machine access details remain private.

Technical details

Python tools manage conversation summaries, searchable wiki pages, computer health checks, and AI program controls across Windows and Linux. Knowledge, hardware control, and automatic workers are kept separate.
